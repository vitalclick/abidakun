Here is a sentence from South Africa's Public Holidays Act:

"Whenever any public holiday falls on a Sunday, the following Monday shall be a public holiday."

Here is the equivalent sentence from Nigeria's:

"If any day appointed to be a public holiday falls on a Saturday or a Sunday, only the Saturday or Sunday concerned and no other day in lieu... shall be kept as a public holiday."

Same question - what happens when a holiday lands on a weekend. Opposite answers. South Africa's law manufactures an extra day off. Nigeria's law explicitly refuses to.

Build a school calendar against South Africa's rule and sell it into Lagos with a currency symbol swapped, and you have invented a school closure that Nigerian law does not grant. An attendance register marked "closed" on a day the statute says is a normal school day is not a rounding error.

It gets less computable from there:

→ Ghana has no fixed substitution rule at all. Under a 2025 amendment, a holiday landing on a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday can be moved to the Friday or the Monday - by presidential order, decided fresh each year. This year Constitution Day (Wed 7 Jan) was observed on Friday 9 Jan. There is no way to compute that in advance. You read the government's own notice.

→ Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, public holidays in Nigeria and Ghana, are set by the sighting of the moon, declared one or two days ahead. No calendar algorithm reproduces a moon sighting. A "full year" calendar shown to a school in January is either quietly guessing or silently missing its biggest days.

→ Even the list itself moves. The same Ghanaian amendment that introduced the discretionary rule also restored one holiday, repealed another, and reinstated a third - four line-item changes to one country's calendar, in one act, in one year.

Kenya and South Africa happen to land on the same substitution rule. That is not one rule shared by two countries - it is two separate statutes read independently that turned out to agree. Getting the same answer by assumption and getting it by verification are not the same piece of work.
