There is one field on every school management system built for this continent that tells you whether it was really built for this continent.

The teacher's professional registration number.

In South Africa that is a SACE number. Reasonable - South Africa has the most mature school software market here, and it is where most of these products start.

Now put that system in front of a school in Lagos.

A Nigerian teacher has no SACE number. They have a TRCN licence, renewed every three years, and teaching without one is an offence.
In Accra it is an NTC licence.
In Nairobi it is a TSC certificate, required in private schools as well as public ones.

Four countries. Four compulsory registration regimes. Four different councils. One field.

If your system asks a Lagos teacher for a SACE number, you have not localised anything. You have translated the currency symbol.

And that field is the small tell. Behind it:

→ Nigeria and Ghana are neighbours, both run a September-to-July year, and both call a year level a "class". But Nigeria says session and Ghana says academic year.

→ Kenya shares Nigeria's three terms, then runs January to November inside one calendar year like South Africa, and says grade.

→ Nigeria and Ghana both sit WASSCE, from the same examinations council. They are not the same examination - different national syllabuses, different core subjects.

There is no regional rule. There are four answers, and the only way to get them is to read what each authority actually publishes.

We build IEMS for South Africa, Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya. Every one of those markets gets its own curriculum model, its own calendar, its own vocabulary, its own tax treatment - and the model tells the school how far it has been checked, rather than presenting itself as authoritative by omission.
